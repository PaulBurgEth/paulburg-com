I used to spend hours reformatting, summarizing, and reorganizing information across tools. Then I built one prompt that I reuse everywhere — and it collapsed that time to almost nothing.

## The pattern

The idea is simple: instead of writing a new prompt every time you open ChatGPT or Claude, you write one that structures *how the AI behaves* for your specific workflow. Not "summarize this" — but "you are my research assistant, you always output in this format, you always flag uncertainty, you never pad responses."

That's a system prompt. And once you write a good one, you never go back.

## What changed

- Email replies: from 12 minutes average to under 3
- Meeting notes: structured summaries in 30 seconds instead of 20 minutes
- Research: no more re-explaining context every session

The compounding effect is real. Every tool I use now gets a custom system prompt. I wrote them once, I maintain them occasionally, and they run in the background.

## The actual prompt

I can't share the full version — it's tuned to my specific workflow — but the structure is:

1. **Role definition** — what the AI is in this context
2. **Output format** — always the same structure
3. **Constraints** — what it must never do
4. **Context** — what it always knows about me

Write those four sections, test for a week, refine. That's it.

The ROI on one hour of prompt engineering is measured in weeks of recovered time.

A year ago I was in Thailand, where I'd been for two years. The plan was to stay. Then I spent a week in Da Nang visiting a friend, and something clicked.

## What I didn't expect

Vietnam is often framed as a budget destination or a stopover. Da Nang is neither. It's a functioning city — infrastructure that works, a coastline that's genuinely beautiful, and a pace that's fast without being exhausting.

What I noticed most: the city is being built in real time. Roads, buildings, cafes, coworking spaces — everything is newer, and newer usually means faster. Internet that doesn't fail. Power that stays on.

For someone building software and running calls across time zones, that matters more than I expected.

## What changed in my work

Moving is always a disruption. I lost two weeks to logistics. But once I settled, the output went up.

Part of that is the timezone — UTC+7 works well for both Europe (mornings) and Southeast Asia. Part of it is the environment: fewer social obligations than Bangkok, more physical space than Bali.

But mostly it's that I stopped optimizing for where I was. Da Nang is neutral ground. I'm not here to experience Vietnam — I'm here to build things. That clarity is underrated.

## Would I recommend it?

For builders: yes, especially if you're tired of the Bali circuit or the Chiang Mai loop. It's not yet overrun. The infrastructure is real. The cost is still low by any measure.

It won't stay this way. But right now it works.
